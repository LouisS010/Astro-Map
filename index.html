Upcoming space events

Location not set — showing each eclipse's maximum coverage instead

Dates and coverage from NASA's eclipse catalogue. "Coverage" is the maximum fraction of the Sun's disc covered — reached only along the narrow path stated for each eclipse; a partial eclipse with lower coverage is visible over a much wider area around it.

Location-based coverage is a rough estimate — a straight-line falloff from the eclipse path, not a precise circumstances calculator. Equinox, solstice, and planetary-opposition dates don't vary by location.

Aurora chance uses NOAA's live geomagnetic Kp-index and their published rule of thumb (the auroral boundary sits at roughly 66° − 2×Kp geomagnetic latitude) — a real but approximate relationship NOAA itself describes as "an average," not a precise forecast. Cloud cover and light pollution matter just as much as the Kp index.
